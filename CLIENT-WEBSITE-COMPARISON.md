# A stronger online home for the Linden Herald

**The same local newspaper. A clearer path from visiting to subscribing, calling, or placing a notice.**

The Linden Herald has served its community since 1959. The proposed website preserves that identity—the familiar name, newspaper imagery, traditional typography and teal color—while making its services easier to discover and use on phones and computers.

The current website introduces the paper and provides its essential information. The new design builds on that foundation by giving visitors clearer choices, more readable pages and a more helpful next step.

## What improves for your readers and your business

| Area | Current website | Proposed website | Practical benefit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phone experience | At the phone width reviewed, the homepage image extends beyond the screen. Navigation uses small text links that wrap across lines. | Content fits the screen, with a mobile menu and prominent Subscribe and Call buttons. | Visitors can read and navigate more comfortably from their phones. |
| First impression | The homepage offers an introduction and a newspaper image. Services are primarily reached through the navigation. | The homepage introduces subscriptions, display advertising, legal notices and the archive, each with a direct next step. | New visitors can quickly see what the Herald offers and where to go. |
| Readability | Small body text and dense paragraphs carry much of the information. | Larger body text, clearer headings, grouped information and left-aligned longer paragraphs on mobile. | Prices, instructions and contact information are easier to scan. |
| Subscriptions | The annual price and mailing instructions are presented in paragraphs. | A prominent annual offer, numbered instructions, a clearly presented mailing address and a copy-address button. | Readers can understand the offer and prepare their subscription more easily. |
| Legal notices | Several services, prices and conditions are combined in one paragraph. | Separate price rows, section links, publication steps and expandable answers to common questions. | Customers can find the relevant information and arrive better prepared to inquire. |
| Getting in touch | Phone numbers appear as plain text on the subscription, advertising and contact pages reviewed. | Tap-to-call links, a persistent Call action on phones and contact information in the footer. | Mobile visitors have a direct way to call without copying the number. |
| Newspaper archive | A list of dated PDF links with file sizes. | Illustrated front-page cards with readable dates, file sizes and a clear indication that PDFs open in another tab. | Readers can recognize an issue visually and browse more confidently. |
| Consistency | Basic page navigation and a copyright footer. | Consistent service links, contact details and navigation throughout the site. | Visitors have useful places to go when they reach the end of a page. |

## Built around the way the Herald already works

The proposed site supports the paper's existing print subscription, telephone and mail-based services. It presents those services more clearly without requiring an online newsroom, daily web articles or an online checkout.

The design also keeps the Herald's familiar character. It gives the paper a more polished online presence while remaining recognizable to its readers.

These improvements are intended to make subscription and advertising inquiries easier. Actual results would be measured after launch; increased sales, traffic or search rankings are not being promised.

## A short introduction for the client meeting

“We built this around what your readers and customers need most: finding out about the paper, subscribing, calling you, placing a legal notice and browsing past issues. We kept the Herald's familiar identity and made those everyday tasks easier—especially on a phone. The new layout makes your services more visible, your prices easier to understand and your contact information easier to use. It gives the newspaper a website that better reflects the care you put into serving the community.”

## Before launch

- Confirm advertising and legal-notice prices, subscription timing, advertising options and phone availability with the owner. The $42 annual subscription price for 52 issues is confirmed.
- Connect and test real contact-message delivery. The proposed form currently demonstrates the experience and does not send messages; the current site's message delivery was not tested.
- Bring the archive up to date. The current site lists September 10, 17 and 24, 2026 issues that are not yet included in the demo. The demo improves archive presentation, but is not currently the more complete archive.
- Update the demo privacy notice to reflect the final hosting and message-handling arrangements.
